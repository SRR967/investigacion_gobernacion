ABSTRACT
In this paper, we present an initiative of the Colombian
Government aiming at implementing interoperability as a service
for integrating public agencies’ services and facilitating
information exchange, publication and consumption.

In order to tackle this problem and to transform the Colombian
Government to function as a single efficient institution, that will
provide timely information to its citizens, streamlined procedures
and better services, public agencies must be connected and
integrated in an articulated and coordinated manner and operate as
a single large system. [1]

Over the past years, the Colombian Government have made many
efforts for facilitating and promoting information exchange
between public agencies through the development of government
programs such as the e-Government Strategy and the
Interoperability Framework. However, and in line with current
technologies and existing paradigms regarding to cloud
computing, the Colombian Government has been designing the
interoperability as a service strategy, as a new model for
achieving effective citizen-centered interoperability, in which

1. INTRODUCTION
Nowadays, public agencies in Colombia operate as information
silos, which means that each agency designs, develops and offers
its own services and procedures individually, without taking into
account the needs for integration with other public agencies’
services. When a service or procedure requires information from
another agency in order to be completed, the citizens are the ones
who end up connecting the public agencies’ services going
physically from one public agency to another. [1]
The Colombian Government has insufficient capabilities for
transferring information, data and knowledge among public
agencies to offer citizen-focused services. This occurs because
public agencies do not have all the information required to attend
citizens, there is not citizen-focused exchange of information, and
there is a need for effective information exchange strategies and
mechanisms.

In this paper, we present an initiative of the Colombian
Government aiming at implementing interoperability as a service
for integrating public agencies’ services and facilitating
information exchange, publication and consumption. In the next
section we provide information about the context in which the
initiative takes place. In section 3 we present a comparison of
some related work developed in other countries. In section 4 we
detail the interoperability as a service strategy. In section 5 we
mention the conclusions.

4.1 Overview
The interoperability between public agencies is one of the main
enablers of e-government progress in Colombia.
In order to make public agencies interoperable, the Colombian
Government must develop the following: (1) encouraging public
agencies to publish information, ensuring its quality, accuracy,
integrity, updating and availability; (2) supporting consumers in
the use of published information; (3) securing that technology
allows the communication between providers and consumers, in
an adaptable manner, connecting people, processes and
information, through heterogeneous environments (physical,
virtual, in the cloud), and allowing agile development; and (4)
defining the legal framework and standards and securing its
fulfillment.

4.3 Business model
Interoperability as a service is a service pack that can be acquired
by public agencies letting them to: (1) access to the
interoperability platform for information services publication, (2)
install information services in the interoperability platform, (3)
access to specialized accompaniment and support for using
information services, (4) consume information services through
the interoperability platform, (5) access to specialized
accompaniment and support for developing information services,
and (6) data virtualization as an optional feature.
The business model of the interoperability as a service initiative is
composed by the following: (1) Value propositions: provide
information exchange services and support its publication and
consumption; (2) Key activities: platform deployment, service
life-cycle management, service monitoring, service pricing,
incident management and publication support; (3) Key resources:
GNAP, interoperability service, portal provider, operational and
commercial staff and financial resources; (4) Key partners:
platform manufacturers, Ministry of ICT and information
providers; (5) Customer relationship: marketing and sales staff,
support staff and help desk; (6) Channels: interoperability
platform, provider portal and support staff; (7) Customer
segments: public agencies – around 7000 for Government-toGovernment services, private enterprises – around 5000 for
Government-to-Business services and citizen folder providers; (8)
Cost structure: operational and commercial staff salaries,
interoperability platform and GNAP costs, portal costs, support
costs and management costs; (9) Revenue streams: payment per
enabled service, payment per additional services and payment per
transaction (compensation scheme); and (10) Societal Benefits:
saving time, opportunity in the delivery of information, reliable
information from the source, integrating services, saving in
transportation, meal and so on and information Security.

4.4 Principles
Interoperability as a service is guided by the following set of
principles: (1) incremental growth, (2) technological neutrality,
(3) federation, (4) reuse, (5) scalability and (6) self-sustainability.

Figure 2. Expected future state of interoperability
In this new interoperability model, public agencies and industry
institutions, as actors of the Colombian Government productive
ecosystem, join together and exchange information through
multiple integration and interoperability platforms of different
manufacturers, connected in a federated model, with the adoption
of common standards and guidelines that clearly state the rules for
exchanging information.

4.2 Methodology
The methodological approximation for implementing the
interoperability as a service strategy, is composed by the
following: (1) a business model that defines the viability of the
project and contains the principles, objectives, canvas model,
related work, general conditions and activities; (2) a service model
that specifies the interoperability service and contains the general
interoperability service architecture, service quality criteria and
service enabling phases; (3) the call for proposals model that
includes the calling and contractual process and contains the
market study, reference terms and operators selection; and (4)
management model that defines the interoperability service
provision and contains the service enabling, operation model,
support model and service use overcrowding model.

supporting activities.

The promotion process refers to promoting and encouraging the
use of the interoperability service by information producers and
consumers. This includes the development of the following
activities: (1) Inform: tell public agencies about the
interoperability service and its benefits; (2) Understand: make
public agencies appropriate interoperability concepts, create an
inventory of information services candidates to be published in
the interoperability service, define the required services to be
published (roadmap) for each public agency; (3) Locate: assess
the current state of the services to be published, and evaluate the
necessary activities required to consume the required services; (4)
Act: identify the responsible people for developing publication
and consumption web services, advise public agencies on the use
of interoperability service, support the definition of working
plans, and track working plans; (5) Encourage information
providers: support the development of information services.

The implementation of interoperability as a service, implies cost
savings for public agencies due to the use of a shared platform
that avoids unnecessary investments in infrastructure and
middleware software, and shared resource management that
impact on reducing the load and operating costs as well as
increased operational efficiency, which at the end is reflected in
more and better services to citizens. Furthermore, it represents an
opportunity for operators to access and develop information
services and earn money through its consumption.
This model requires manufacturers to develop an initial effort to
jointly develop a scalable, reliable and functional architecture, and
provide integral services, ranging from supporting public agencies
in information services definition and discovery, and enabling
them into the corresponding platforms. It is important to
understand that information services are the result of internal
integration, information systems redesigns, and infrastructure
refinement processes.

Finally, the use process refers to using the interoperability service
by information producers and consumers. This includes the
development of the following activities: (1) publish information
services, (2) consume information services, and (3) develop valueadded services.

The coordination process refers to organizing the efforts and
resources that lead to the achievement of interoperability in the
Colombian Government. This includes the development of the
following activities: (1) monitor the activities of authorization,
operation and promotion of the interoperability service, (2) define
governance scheme such as the interoperability committee, (3)
establish the roadmap resolution, (4) set rules and enforce them,
(5) manage financial resources, (6) search self-sustainability, (7)
manage information service providers and (8) manage information
services consumers.

The required capabilities of the platforms that support the
interoperability as a service initiative are the following: (1) a
service directory that contains all available services; (2)
management capabilities, including logs, audit, monitoring and
service management; (3) composition capabilities, including
orchestration, business processes and inter-institutional service
procedures; (4) connection and transport capabilities, including
application program interfaces, adapters and messaging; (5)
security capabilities, including timestamp, digital signatures,
authentication and authorization; (6) management and monitoring
portal; (7) information portal; and (8) value-added services,
including online payments, electronic notification of
administrative acts and citizen folder.

The implementation process refers to installing and operating the
interoperability service. This includes the development of the
following activities: (1) ensure necessary technological resources
(connectivity, hardware, database and middleware software), (2)
install, configure and deploy platform, (3) verify the correct
operation of the platform and other components of the
interoperability service (portal and table service).

4.9 Financial Model
Aiming at building up a sustainable interoperability in Colombia,
a financial model has been developed which seeks to address the
following questions: (1) number of payable transactions to be
executed in the interoperability platform to make business viable,
(2) estimated price per transaction, (3) payback period under
transactional and price conditions, (4) service duration to make
business viable, and (5) provider margin under transactional and
price conditions.

4.10 Benefits, Barriers and Success Factors
Some of the main benefits that can be obtained through the
implementation of the interoperability as a service initiative are
